Appendix

How to Choose Between the Main Model Routes

This appendix is for the question that appears after you already have an RTD and chemistry: should you stay with the direct RTD route, switch to Tanks-in-Series, use Dispersion Model, or move into the Tab 5 workspace? The answer depends less on which tab looks more advanced and more on which engineering question you are trying to answer.

Decision Matrix

Route Best when... Main strength Main limitation
Prediction ModelsYou want the first direct reactive reading of the real RTD.Keeps the full RTD rather than compressing it into one surrogate parameter.It is a comparison route, not a compact equivalent-model language.
Tanks-in-SeriesYou want an interpretable one-parameter equivalent based on N.Easy to communicate as progressive backmixing through an equivalent cascade.It compresses the RTD into a surrogate that may hide shape details.
Dispersion ModelYou want a Peclet-based hydrodynamic interpretation and explicit boundary-condition reasoning.Natural language for axial dispersion problems and boundary-condition sensitivity.Needs more care in interpretation and has route-specific restrictions.
Tab 5 workspaceYou want fit, consolidated reactive comparison, or process-side optimization.Keeps design tasks in one place and lets you compare original vs fitted RTD sources.It assumes the upstream RTD and chemistry are already trustworthy.

Typical Use Cases

Stay in Prediction Models when...

  • You want the least compressed reading of how the real RTD affects reaction outcomes.
  • You need to compare Segregation and Maximum Mixedness directly on the original RTD.
  • You are still validating whether the RTD and chemistry together make sense at all.

Move to TIS when...

  • You want a simple equivalent-reactor narrative built around one parameter.
  • You need to discuss non-ideality as a degree of backmixing rather than as a full RTD curve.
  • You are comfortable with a cascade surrogate and do not need every RTD detail preserved explicitly.

Move to Dispersion when...

  • Your problem is naturally framed in axial-dispersion language.
  • You care about the meaning of boundary assumptions.
  • You want to interpret the RTD through Pe / Bo rather than through an equivalent cascade size.

Move to Tab 5 when...

  • You want to fit an equivalent RTD family first and then reuse it in design tasks.
  • You want a four-model comparison inside the same workspace that later hosts Optimization.
  • You want process-side optimization with a fixed RTD rather than hydrodynamic tuning.

Main Tradeoffs to Keep in Mind

Tradeoff What it means
Full RTD vs surrogate modelPrediction Models preserves the full RTD logic; TIS and Dispersion compress that logic into an equivalent interpretation.
Interpretability vs fidelity to shapeEquivalent models are often easier to explain, but they may hide useful RTD-shape details.
Generality vs assumption loadSome routes are broader screening tools, while others introduce stronger interpretive assumptions or kinetic restrictions.
Hydrodynamic reading vs design decisionTabs 2-4 focus on understanding or approximating hydrodynamics; Tab 5 focuses on design-oriented decisions built on top of them.
Most common shortcut to avoid: choosing the most "advanced-looking" tab is not the same as choosing the most appropriate one. A simpler route is often better if it answers the real question more directly.

Typical Selection Mistakes

  • Leaving Prediction Models too early before verifying that the original RTD and chemistry already produce sensible behavior.
  • Using TIS or Dispersion as if they were automatically more rigorous than the direct RTD route.
  • Moving into Optimization when the real uncertainty still sits in RTD interpretation or chemistry definition.
  • Choosing Dispersion Model only because Pe is familiar, without checking whether its boundary assumptions match the problem.
